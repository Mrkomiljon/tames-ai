

# Cover letter

[\[Date\]\]

[\[Editor-in-Chief\]\] \[\[Journal\]\]

Dear \[\[Editor\]\],

We submit the manuscript “TAMES AI: An Adaptive Fuzzy, Multimodal Ensemble Model for Malignancy Risk Assessment of Thyroid Nodules” (Research Article) for consideration in \[\[Journal\]\].

The manuscript describes an uncertainty-aware framework for thyroid-nodule risk assessment from B-mode ultrasound: an adaptive fuzzy image layer that produces a pixel membership map and an image-quality coefficient, a quality-weighted fusion of image embedding and sonographic descriptors, a hybrid ensemble of four learners, and evaluated explanations. It is developed and tested on the public TN3K dataset (3,493 images) with the dataset's official split and a statistical analysis plan fixed before the test set was scored. We report the results as they are: with an expert delineation the model matches but does not exceed an image-only convolutional network (AUC 0.815 vs 0.830, p = 0.10); when the delineation is produced automatically, the setting in which such a system would be used, it keeps its accuracy (0.823) while the network and a conventional late fusion lose theirs (0.805 and 0.785; p = 0.010 after correction for the latter); it gives the best-calibrated probabilities; and under heavy synthetic noise it is the least robust model, which the ablation traces to the quality gating and the fuzzy inputs. All code, configurations, per-image predictions and trained weights are released, and the paper follows TRIPOD+AI and CLAIM.

An earlier version of this work was reviewed at \[\[Journal of the first submission\]\] and not accepted; the reviewers' comments concerned editorial notes left in the text, missing cohort and protocol detail and corrupted equations. The present manuscript was rebuilt from that review; a point-by-point response is attached for the editor's information.

The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere, all authors have approved it, and there are no competing interests. The study used only publicly available de-identified data, so no ethics approval was required. We suggest no exclusions of reviewers.

Sincerely,

[\[Corresponding author, affiliation, e-mail, ORCID\]\], on behalf of both authors

# Response to reviewers

Manuscript ID: \[\[ \]\] · Title: TAMES AI: An Adaptive Fuzzy, Multimodal Ensemble Model for Malignancy Risk Assessment of Thyroid Nodules (previously “TAMES AI: An Artificial Intelligence Model Based on Adaptive Fuzzy Set Theory and Hybrid Machine Learning Algorithms for Early Detection of Thyroid Cancer”) · Journal: \[\[ \]\]

Dear \[\[Editor\]\],

We thank the editor and the reviewers for a careful reading. We accept every point raised. The version we submitted contained internal editorial notes that should never have reached the journal, its Methods did not allow the work to be reproduced, and its reported results were not backed by a documented experiment. We have rebuilt the study around an experiment that is fully specified and reproducible from a public repository: the model is developed and tested on the public TN3K dataset with its official split, every equation is defined in one notation, and every number in Section 5 is produced by the released code. The results are more modest than those first reported and we state them as they are: with an expert delineation the model matches but does not exceed an image-only network; without one it keeps its accuracy while the comparators lose theirs; under heavy noise it is the least robust model, and we explain why. The title was changed to describe what the study does. Because the manuscript was rewritten in full, a tracked-changes comparison would not be readable; we supply a clean version, and the table below gives the location of every change.
